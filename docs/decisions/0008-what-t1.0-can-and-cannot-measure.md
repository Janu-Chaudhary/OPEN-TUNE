# 0008 — AC7 fails and is fixable; AC2 is not measurable with algorithm-derived labels
**Date:** 2026-09-04 · **Resolves:** how to read T1.0's AC2/AC7 result · **Status:** accepted

## Context
T1.0 built a reference set from the owner's six sung Hindi takes, labelled by 2-of-3 consensus
across three independent Python estimators, and scored `YinDetector` against it. Headline:
**AC2 83.0% (needs 95%), AC7 8.08% octave errors (needs <1%)** — both failing, after YIN
measured 0.375 cents on synthetic sines. This record is the diagnosis.

All figures below are the controller's own, on take04 (the hardest take), matched at the
23.09 ms alignment the scorer uses.

## Finding 1 — the velocity hypothesis was wrong
The obvious explanation was that octave errors cluster during *meend*: YIN analyses a fixed
~46 ms window, and pitch that slides within the window smears the difference function. Measured:

| Label pitch velocity | frames | within ±15¢ | octave error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–150 ¢/s (steady) | 1621 | 43.4% | **16.16%** |
| 400–900 | 2122 | 32.5% | 13.71% |
| 2000+ ¢/s (fast slide) | 936 | 10.8% | **11.86%** |

**Octave errors are flat across velocity — slightly worse on steady notes.** Slides are not the
cause. Velocity *does* wreck fine accuracy (43% → 11%), which is window smearing, but that is a
precision effect and a different problem. The two were being conflated.

## Finding 2 — the detector's own confidence predicts its errors
| YIN confidence | frames | within ±15¢ | octave error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.70–0.85 | 885 | 15.6% | 18.53% |
| 0.85–0.95 | 4370 | 25.0% | 19.59% |
| 0.95–1.00 | 3052 | 47.2% | **5.37%** |

The aperiodicity measure already separates good frames from bad. The engine accepts the bad ones
as voiced, because T1.7's threshold (0.2 aperiodicity, provisional) is far too loose.

## Finding 3 — AC7 is reachable, at a real cost
| conf ≥ | coverage | octave error | AC7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.80 (current) | 100% | 14.25% | fail |
| 0.95 | 36.7% | 5.37% | fail |
| **0.98** | **14.6%** | **0.91%** | **pass** |
| 0.995 | 1.6% | 0.00% | pass |

Tightening the voicing threshold buys AC7 and keeps ~15% of frames. That is a genuine trade,
not a free fix: 85% of the performance becomes unvoiced and passes through uncorrected (FR2).
Whether that is acceptable is a listening question, not a numbers one.

## Finding 4 — AC2 cannot be measured with these labels, and that is the important one
Inter-estimator spread on frames the labels call **agreed**:

| | cents |
|---|---|
| median | 8.37 |
| p75 | 15.22 |
| p95 | 25.78 |

**On 25.7% of agreed frames the three estimators disagree with each other by more than 15
cents** — the very tolerance AC2 asks us to measure. The instrument's resolution is the same
size as the quantity. The reported "AC2 83%" is therefore **not a failure of the detector; it
is a measurement at the edge of the ruler.** It is neither a pass nor a fail.

AC7 is unaffected by this: an octave is 1200 cents, roughly 100× the label noise, so an octave
error is unambiguous even against imprecise labels.

## Decision
1. **Treat AC7 as genuinely failing** and act on it. The threshold is the lever; T1.7's 0.2 is
   provisional and should be re-derived, weighing coverage against octave rate.
2. **Treat AC2 as unmeasured**, not failed. Do not report a number from this set as if it
   settled the criterion, and do not tune the detector to improve it — that would be fitting to
   label noise.
3. **To measure AC2 properly** needs ground truth an order of magnitude tighter than the target:
   a laryngograph (none available for Hindi singing), or synthetic voices where f0 is exact by
   construction. The synthetic route is available to us today and should carry AC2.

## Consequences
- `specs.md` AC2 needs a note stating what can and cannot establish it, or the next reader
  repeats this work.
- T1.8 splits: AC1 and AC7 are answerable now; AC2 waits on synthetic ground truth.
- The general lesson, and it has bitten this project before: **check the instrument's resolution
  before believing the measurement.** See `docs/lessons.md`.
