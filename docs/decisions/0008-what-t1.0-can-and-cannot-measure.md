# 0008 — AC7 fails and is fixable; AC2 is not measurable with algorithm-derived labels
**Date:** 2026-09-04 · **Resolves:** how to read T1.0's AC2/AC7 result · **Status:** accepted

## Context
T1.0 built a reference set from the owner's six sung Hindi takes, labelled by 2-of-3 consensus
across three independent Python estimators, and scored `YinDetector` against it. Headline:
**AC2 83.0% (needs 95%), AC7 8.08% octave errors (needs <1%)** — both failing, after YIN
measured 0.375 cents on synthetic sines. This record is the diagnosis.

All figures below are the controller's own, on take04 (the hardest take), matched at the
23.09 ms alignment the scorer uses.

## Finding 1 — the velocity hypothesis was wrong
The obvious explanation was that octave errors cluster during *meend*: YIN analyses a fixed
~46 ms window, and pitch that slides within the window smears the difference function. Measured:

| Label pitch velocity | frames | within ±15¢ | octave error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–150 ¢/s (steady) | 1621 | 43.4% | **16.16%** |
| 400–900 | 2122 | 32.5% | 13.71% |
| 2000+ ¢/s (fast slide) | 936 | 10.8% | **11.86%** |

**Octave errors are flat across velocity — slightly worse on steady notes.** Slides are not the
cause. That half stands.

**The other half was wrong, and is corrected here (2026-09-04, after the synthetic set landed).**
This record read the 43% → 11% collapse in fine accuracy as YIN smearing across its window. It is
not. On synthetic voices with *exact* labels, YIN's median error at 3500 cents/second is **1.49
cents** — no velocity effect at all. What degrades on the real takes is the **labels**: the three
consensus estimators are window-based too, and they smear during slides exactly as any such method
must.

| Label velocity | frames | median label spread | frames whose estimators disagree >15 ¢ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–150 ¢/s | 2219 | 6.21 ¢ | 16.3% |
| 900–2000 | 2076 | 11.65 ¢ | 35.1% |
| 2000+ | 1896 | **13.42 ¢** | **44.5%** |

In the fastest bin nearly half the "agreed" labels are internally inconsistent by more than AC2's
entire tolerance. The apparent finding "YIN gets worse as pitch moves" was the ruler getting worse
as pitch moves — L5 again, one layer deeper, in the very record that first stated L5.

For completeness: the synthetic agent proposed a different cause — that the scorer's 23.09 ms
alignment should be ~30.25 ms. Re-running these bins at both offsets changes the numbers by under
2 points and leaves the gradient intact, so alignment is **not** the explanation. The offset
correction is real and worth making on its own merits; it is not this.

## Finding 2 — the detector's own confidence predicts its errors
| YIN confidence | frames | within ±15¢ | octave error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.70–0.85 | 885 | 15.6% | 18.53% |
| 0.85–0.95 | 4370 | 25.0% | 19.59% |
| 0.95–1.00 | 3052 | 47.2% | **5.37%** |

The aperiodicity measure already separates good frames from bad. The engine accepts the bad ones
as voiced, because T1.7's threshold (0.2 aperiodicity, provisional) is far too loose.

## Finding 3 — AC7 is reachable, at a real cost
| conf ≥ | coverage | octave error | AC7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.80 (current) | 100% | 14.25% | fail |
| 0.95 | 36.7% | 5.37% | fail |
| **0.98** | **14.6%** | **0.91%** | **pass** |
| 0.995 | 1.6% | 0.00% | pass |

Tightening the voicing threshold buys AC7 and keeps ~15% of frames. That is a genuine trade,
not a free fix: 85% of the performance becomes unvoiced and passes through uncorrected (FR2).
Whether that is acceptable is a listening question, not a numbers one.

## Finding 4 — AC2 cannot be measured with these labels, and that is the important one
Inter-estimator spread on frames the labels call **agreed**:

| | cents |
|---|---|
| median | 8.37 |
| p75 | 15.22 |
| p95 | 25.78 |

**On 25.7% of agreed frames the three estimators disagree with each other by more than 15
cents** — the very tolerance AC2 asks us to measure. The instrument's resolution is the same
size as the quantity. The reported "AC2 83%" is therefore **not a failure of the detector; it
is a measurement at the edge of the ruler.** It is neither a pass nor a fail.

AC7 is unaffected by this: an octave is 1200 cents, roughly 100× the label noise, so an octave
error is unambiguous even against imprecise labels.

## Superseding measurement (2026-09-04): the synthetic set answered both criteria

With exact labels — verified to 0.08 cents worst case, ~1000x tighter than the consensus set:

| | YIN | Autocorrelation |
|---|---|---|
| AC1 (±5 ¢) | **0.21 ¢ — PASS** | 1206 ¢ — fail |
| AC2 (95% ≤15 ¢) | **93.99% — fails narrowly** | 87.88% |
| AC7 (<1% octave) | **1.34% — fails** | 8.45% |
| AC6 | 0.00% — pass | 0.00% — pass |

**AC2's failure is localised, not general.** 100% on all 12 steady notes, all five meend speeds
*including 3500 ¢/s*, both vibrato cases, both shimmer cases, weak and missing fundamentals,
telephone band, and both voicing cases. What remains is 2% jitter and the two breathy cases.

**Corrected again 2026-09-04 (D10 implementation): the fallback was only half the mechanism.**
This record named the threshold fallback as the sole cause. Instrumenting the failing frames showed
that at HNR 10 dB the errors come from step 3 *proper*, never reaching the fallback at all:
`d'(P)` ≈ 0.117 sits just above the paper's fixed 0.1 while `d'(2P)` ≈ 0.088 sits just below, so
the first-crossing walk fires **for the first time at the octave**. Every residual frame in an
intermediate experiment carried `cleared = true`. Only HNR 5 dB is a pure fallback failure.

That mattered, because it ruled out the narrow fix: repairing the fallback alone plateaus at 9.19%
on `breathy_hnr10db` at any tolerance. What shipped instead makes the threshold *relative* —
`max(kAbsoluteThreshold, 2.0 × min d')` — which can only raise the bar above 0.1, leaves clean
signals bit-identical, and removes the fallback rather than relocating it, since the global minimum
always qualifies.

**Every one of YIN's octave errors came from breathy phonation, and every one was octave DOWN.**
Excluding those two cases the rate is 0.02%. The mechanism was traced, not guessed: it is step 3's
**fallback**. When no lag clears `kAbsoluteThreshold` = 0.1, `YinDetector` takes the global CMND
minimum — the rule its own comment calls "a coin flip between P, 2P and 3P". Fallback rate is 0%
at HNR 20 dB, 46.6% at 10 dB, and **100% at 5 dB**. On a sampled frame CMND(P) = 0.175 while
CMND(2P) = 0.126, and 0.126 sits comfortably under T1.7's 0.2 voicing gate — so the wrong answer
ships as *confidently voiced*.

## Decision
1. **Treat AC7 as genuinely failing** and act on it — but **not via the voicing threshold.** That
   was this record's original recommendation and the synthetic measurement refutes it: the bad
   frames pass the gate with room to spare. The fix belongs in the fallback rule, which currently
   guesses when it should either widen its search or report unvoiced.
2. **Treat AC2 as unmeasured**, not failed. Do not report a number from this set as if it
   settled the criterion, and do not tune the detector to improve it — that would be fitting to
   label noise.
3. **To measure AC2 properly** needs ground truth an order of magnitude tighter than the target:
   a laryngograph (none available for Hindi singing), or synthetic voices where f0 is exact by
   construction. The synthetic route is available to us today and should carry AC2.

## Consequences
- `specs.md` AC2 needs a note stating what can and cannot establish it, or the next reader
  repeats this work.
- T1.8 splits: AC1 and AC7 are answerable now; AC2 waits on synthetic ground truth.
- The general lesson, and it has bitten this project before: **check the instrument's resolution
  before believing the measurement.** See `docs/lessons.md`.

## Outcome (2026-09-04, after D10 and D11)

Re-measured on the synthetic set with the corrected alignment, excluding the
`even_harmonics_only` behaviour pin (that signal genuinely repeats at P/2, so 2·f0 is the right
answer — see T1.2 and decision 0003):

| | YIN | Autocorrelation |
|---|---|---|
| AC2 (≥95%) | **96.10% — PASS** | 87.51% |
| AC7 (<1%) | **0.000% — PASS** | 8.464% |
| median error | 0.17 ¢ | 2.46 ¢ |

**Zero octave errors on every case.** The two breathy cases went from 29.04% and 27.27% to 0.00%,
and both now sit at 100% within ±15 cents. The whole AC2 residue is two jitter cases at ~49-50%
with median errors of 14.9 and 15.9 cents — 2% cycle-to-cycle jitter means the period genuinely
varies frame to frame, so demanding a single f0 to ±15 cents there is arguably stricter than AC2
intends. That is a question about the criterion, not the detector.

The improvement was **not bought by reclassifying frames as unvoiced**, which is the way this
number is most easily faked: on the frames voiced in both runs, octave errors went 130 → 0 and
within-15 went 10884 → 11127. Voiced fractions are bit-identical on 30 of 33 cases.
